# Project management concepts

These pages explain the ideas behind managing projects: where they come from, what they ask of a team, and when they help. They're about the methods, not about Leantime's buttons. Each one ends with a short section on doing it in Leantime, with links to the how-to pages.

You don't need a method to start. Most teams borrow a few practices that fit, like a board that shows work in progress or a short look back after each stretch of work, and add more when they need them.

## Where should I start?

- **[Lean](knowledge-base/whatislean.md):** deliver what the customer values, and cut the waste around it.
- **[Agile](knowledge-base/agile.md):** work in small steps, check in often, and change course when you learn something.
- **[Scrum](knowledge-base/scrum.md):** a framework for agile work in fixed-length sprints, with set roles and meetings.
- **[Kanban](knowledge-base/kanban.md):** make work visible, limit how much is in progress, and keep it flowing.
- **[Phases of a project](knowledge-base/phasesofproject.md):** the stages most projects go through, and when to plan them all up front.
- **[Design thinking](knowledge-base/idea-development.md):** understand people's needs before deciding what to build.

## How do these methods fit together?

They overlap more than their names suggest. Lean is the oldest of them: it came from manufacturing, and Kanban grew out of it. Agile came from software teams and shares Lean's focus on small batches and fast feedback. Scrum is one way to work in an agile way. Design thinking comes before all of them: it helps you decide what's worth building in the first place.
