For game studios, software and product teams, and startups: you build something, release it, learn from players or users, and build the next version. The setup below keeps releases on a timeline, ideas and feedback in one place, and the reason for each release in view.

## How should a game studio or product team set up Leantime?

| Your team | What to create |
|---|---|
| **One to three people, one product or game** | One project. A milestone per release ("Demo build", "Early access", "1.0"), with its to-dos under it. |
| **A larger team, one product** | A project per area, like "Game client", "Backend" and "Art and audio", if each has its own people and pace. Otherwise keep one project and use milestones. |
| **Several products or games** | A [client](/using-leantime/managing-clients/) per product (the field is called **Client / Product**), with its projects under it. The Program Plans plugin can group the projects of one product line. |

## How do I plan releases with milestones in Leantime?

- **Make each release a milestone** with a due date: the stage ends when the build ships.
- **Keep outcomes small inside a release.** "Level 1 playable" or "Sign-up works" are outcomes you can finish in days, not weeks; track them as to-dos with subtasks, or as smaller milestones if several people share one.
- **Group the board by milestone** to see each release's work together (see [To-Dos](/using-leantime/task-management/)).

## Where do ideas, feedback and playtest notes go?

- **Ideas** holds feature ideas and player requests until you decide what goes into a release (see [Ideas](/using-leantime/idea-management/)).
- **Research boards** keep what you learned from playtests and interviews (see [Research boards](/using-leantime/research-boards/)).
- **Blueprints** like the Lean Canvas or the Project Value Canvas help when you're working out who the product is for (see [Blueprints](/using-leantime/blueprints/)).

## How do I know if a release worked?

- **Give each release a goal you can measure:** wishlists, daily players, crash-free sessions, sign-ups. Link the goal to the release milestone, so you see whether shipping it moved the number (see [Goals](/using-leantime/goals/)).
- **Run a retrospective after each release** to keep what worked (see [Retrospectives](/using-leantime/retrospectives/)).

## Tips for product and game teams

- **Open source and community projects:** set the project to **Everyone in this organization** and give contributors the **Editor** or **Commenter** role.
- **Switch off what you don't use** on the project's **Modules** tab, like Time tracking if nobody bills hours.
