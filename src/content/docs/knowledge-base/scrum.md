Scrum is a framework for doing [agile](/knowledge-base/agile/) work in fixed-length cycles called sprints. The [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) calls it "a lightweight framework that helps people, teams and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems." It's deliberately small: a few roles, a few meetings and a few lists, and the team fills in the rest.

## Where does Scrum come from?

The name comes from rugby. Hirotaka Takeuchi and Ikujiro Nonaka used it in a 1986 Harvard Business Review article, "The New New Product Development Game", for teams that move forward together. Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland shaped it into a framework for software in the 1990s, and still maintain the Scrum Guide. Its current version is from 2020.

## What is a sprint?

A sprint is a fixed stretch of time, "one month or less", in which the team turns a set of work into a finished, usable result. Two weeks is common. A new sprint starts as soon as the last one ends, so the team gets into a steady rhythm.

Keeping sprints the same length makes it easier to plan, and to see how much the team actually gets through.

## Who is on a Scrum team?

A Scrum team is small, "typically 10 or fewer people", and has three accountabilities:

- **Product Owner:** decides what's most valuable, keeps the list of work (the Product Backlog) in order, and is one person, not a committee.
- **Scrum Master:** helps the team and the organization understand and use Scrum, and removes what gets in the team's way. Not a project manager who assigns work.
- **Developers:** the people doing the work, whatever their skills. They decide how to do it.

There are no sub-teams or hierarchy inside a Scrum team. The team manages itself.

## What are the Scrum events?

| Event | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| **The Sprint** | The container for all the other events |
| **Sprint Planning** | Decide why this sprint matters (the Sprint Goal), what to take on, and how |
| **Daily Scrum** | A 15-minute check-in for the developers, same time every day, to adjust the plan toward the Sprint Goal |
| **Sprint Review** | Show the result to the people it's for, and decide what to do next |
| **Sprint Retrospective** | Look at how the team worked, and pick changes to make next time |

## What are the Scrum artifacts?

- **Product Backlog:** everything that might be done, in order of value. Its commitment is the **Product Goal**: the longer-term objective.
- **Sprint Backlog:** what the team takes on this sprint, and the plan to get it done. Its commitment is the **Sprint Goal**.
- **Increment:** the finished result. Its commitment is the **Definition of Done**: the quality bar every piece of work has to meet.

## What about story points and velocity?

These aren't part of Scrum itself, but many Scrum teams use them.

- **Estimating effort:** instead of guessing hours, teams compare work by size: "this is about twice as big as that". Story points and T-shirt sizes (S, M, L, XL) are two ways to do it. Estimates improve as the team learns what a "Large" really takes.
- **Velocity:** how much estimated work the team finishes in a sprint, on average. It helps plan the next sprint. It's a planning aid for the team, not a score to compare teams by.

## Doing it in Leantime

- **Sprints:** create a sprint from the menu next to **To-Dos**, and put to-dos in it. The rest wait in the **Backlog**. See [To-Dos](/using-leantime/task-management/).
- **Estimating:** each to-do's **Effort** is a size, from "< 2min" and XS to XXL.
- **Daily Scrum:** the To-Dos [Kanban board](/using-leantime/task-management/), showing the sprint.
- **Burndown:** with sprints in use, **Delivery Metrics** in [Reports](/using-leantime/reports/) shows a Sprint Burndown.
- **Velocity:** Leantime doesn't calculate it. To plan the next sprint, look at the **Effort** of the to-dos finished in recent ones, for example "3 XL, 2 M and 1 S".
- **Retrospective:** the **Retrospectives** blueprint has Continue, Stop and Start columns. See [Retrospectives](/using-leantime/retrospectives/).
- **Roles:** Leantime's user roles are about permissions, not Scrum accountabilities. A Product Owner or Scrum Master can have any role that lets them do their part.
